# **COMMUNIST PARTY OF INDIA (MARXIST) Central Committee 
## **Report on Political Developments** 

_(Adopted at the Central Committee meeting held on May 22-24 at New Delhi)_ 

Many important national and international developments took place in the intervening period between the last meeting of the Central Committee held in January and May. Some of the main trends are dealt with in this report. 

## **International** 

The most significant features of the international developments in the last four months are: 

- i. The US-Israel attacks on Iran mark a new stage of imperialist aggression. They show to what lengths imperialists would go to overcome the current crisis and demonstrate their hegemony, despite various constraining factors; 

- ii. Imperialism is desperately moving to dismantle the post Second World War world order, including the UN, by trampling upon the independence and sovereignty of countries and institutions; 

- iii. The US is moving aggressively against socialist Cuba; 

- iv. The people, government and the Communist Party of Cuba are staunchly resisting all US attempts to destabilise the country and enforce a regime change; 

- v. A conflict within NATO and between the US and its allies can be noticed; 

- vi. The US attack on Iran threw the global economy into a turmoil, with rising prices, fertilizer and food scarcity; 

- vii.Attacks on workers' rights in the form of reforming labour laws to enable corporate profits are being resisted in all parts of the world; 

- viii. Despite these class actions, the far-right continues to grow; 

- ix. There is an urgent need for communist and workers’ parties to strengthen their forces and stand in solidarity with Cuba, Palestine and other peoples who are resisting imperialist attacks.

# **Imperialist Aggression** 

**West Asia:** The US and Israel launched their attack on Iran on February 28, and though the initial intensity of the attacks has subsided, they are continuing. In the initial phase of attacks, the entire top leadership of Iran, including Supreme Leader Ayatollah Khamenei, was killed in targeted bombings. A school for girls was deliberately targeted, which led to the deaths of 168 children who were in their classrooms. More than 7,000 people were killed in Iran, and thousands more were injured. The US deliberately targeted Iran's infrastructure with the intention of destroying its economic life. 

Backing the US, Israel re-launched its offensive on Lebanon and killed more than 3,000 people in some of the most devastating attacks on the country. Iran retaliated with force and launched attacks on US bases in the region and also on Israel. Hezbollah too attacked Israel, proving wrong all those who had declared the organisation to have been finished by Israel in its previous attacks on Lebanon. Though unreported by many media organisations, Iran inflicted serious damage on Israel and on US allies in West Asia. 

Iran had blocked the Strait of Hormuz, through which 20 percent of the world's energy resources move. This had a severe impact on the world economy, and the US was forced to negotiate. The talks between the two countries through mediators are continuing. The statements of the US President and the attitude of the US administration are not helping to advance the talks or reach a conclusive settlement. The US imposed its own blockade outside the Strait and declared that it would not allow any Iranian ships to exit the Strait. Attacks on ships in the region are being carried out by both parties, escalating tensions. 

The major features arising from this war are: (i) the refusal of NATO countries to join the US war efforts; (ii) NATO countries like Spain, France, and Italy even refused to allow US fighter planes to use their airspace; (iii) the resilience of Iran and its people in resisting US-Israel aggression; (iv) the exposure of the limitations of US war strategy and dominance; (v) the exponential rise in the price of oil and natural gas (before the war, one barrel of oil was $72 and after the war it touched a peak of $120, now hovering around $100 now) adversely impacting the global economy; and (vi) the large-scale opposition to US aggression from all over the world, particularly from within the US, reflected in the falling popularity ratings of President Donald Trump. 

**Palestine:** Israel continues its attacks on Gaza and the West Bank in Palestine. The situation in Gaza is extremely bleak despite the ceasefire. Israel has passed a law allowing the death penalty for Palestinian prisoners. The Israeli Defence Forces (IDF) are killing Palestinians in Gaza with impunity. Whatever schools and hospitals remain are being bombed. Hunger, malnutrition and starvation are rampant, as Israel is now controlling the amount of aid reaching Gaza. Pregnant and lactating women and infants are the worst affected by the lack of food. 

2

One child is killed by the Israeli forces every week in the West Bank, Palestine. The destruction of houses, agricultural lands and properties owned by Palestinians in the West Bank to enable Israeli settlers is continuing unabated. 

Israel has illegally detained peace activists who carried aid in flotillas to distribute in Gaza. The IDF hijacked the flotillas from international waters and prevented the aid from being distributed. It is only upon international pressure that the activists were released. 

**Threats to Cuba:** The US has yet again announced that it will ‘take over’ Cuba and has further tightened sanctions. It is threatening a military invasion of Cuba. The US is flying drones and reconnaissance flights around Cuba, with the intention of threatening it. The US naval carrier Nimitz was sent to the Caribbean sea. These acts are very similar to what it had done before attacking Venezuela and abducting President Nicolas Maduro. Former President and revolutionary leader Raul Castro is indicted by a US Court on false charges. 

On 1 May, the US passed an order declaring mandatory secondary sanctions against any foreign entity that operates or conducts transactions with Cuban entities. This order is an instrument of direct coercion against sovereign third-party States and threatens the global financial system. The oil blockade continues, and as a result, Cuba continues to suffer from a severe energy crisis. China denounced the sanctions and opposed interference in Cuba's internal affairs. It expressed its firm support for Cuba in safeguarding its national sovereignty and security. The European Union, Germany, Mexico, Spain, and Brazil have also criticised the US threats, rejected military intervention, and called for a dialogue. 

**Anti-War Protests:** During this period large anti-war protests took place across multiple regions, against the war on Gaza and Iran waged by the US and Israel. Students, workers and women, played an active role in organising these protests. Port workers in Greece, Spain, Italy, Turkey and Morocco protested against the shipment of armaments to Israel. Huge anti-war protests were held in Australia and the US against the attack on Iran. In May, an international Gaza aid flotilla involving more than 400 activists from over 40 countries attempted to break Israel’s blockade and distribute aid in Gaza. The flotilla’s interception by Israeli forces in international waters, triggered protests in Europe and other regions. The call for peace by Pope Leo XIV also contributed to the spread of anti-war movement. 

**US-China Talks:** US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping met in Beijing for extensive discussions from 13 to 15 May. The decade since the two leaders last met in Beijing has witnessed the emergence of a far more assertive China, which was evident during these discussions. The meeting assumes significance against the backdrop of a volatile geopolitical environment and multiple crises confronting the world. No joint statement was issued after the meeting, indicating the tense nature of relations between the two countries. 

3

According to statements released by both countries, the discussions covered several issues beyond trade and bilateral relations. The conflicts in West Asia and Ukraine, as well as tensions on the Korean Peninsula, were also discussed. The Chinese statement noted that both presidents agreed on a new vision for building a constructive China-US relationship based on strategic stability. This framework is intended to provide strategic guidance for bilateral relations over the next three years and beyond, promote the steady, sound, and sustainable development of China-US relations, and contribute to global peace, prosperity, and progress. 

China strongly reiterated its position on Taiwan and stated that it would not tolerate any external interference. It also emphasised that if the Taiwan issue is handled appropriately, bilateral relations would remain stable. Otherwise, the two countries could face clashes and even conflict, placing the overall relationship in serious jeopardy. The Chinese President also hoped that tensions between a rising superpower and a declining power should not escalate into conflict, as such an outcome would not serve the interests of the world. 

Following the meeting between the Chinese and US presidents in 2025, six rounds of delegation-level talks were held before the leaders met again in Beijing. More than 7,000 Chinese-funded enterprises operate in the United States, while around 80,000 American-invested enterprises are active in China. Therefore, despite growing strains in their relationship, it remains important for both countries to maintain strong trade ties. 

China did not commit to the US request to intervene in its war with Iran. Instead, it stated that the conflict should end immediately and that Iran had not initiated the war. However, China agreed that the Strait of Hormuz should remain open for global shipping. If persuading China to act in support of the US position in the war was the primary objective of the US President’s visit, the effort appears to have failed. 

**China-Russia Relations:** Immediately after the visit of US President Donald Trump, Russian President Vladmir Putin visited Beijing and held discussions with Xi Jinping. Both the countries released a Joint Declaration on the ‘Establishment of a Multipolar World and a New Type of International Relations’. They agreed to counter any manifestations of ‘unilateralism and hegemony’, ‘provocative actions’ and ‘reviving the signs of fascism and militarism’. The further strengthening of relations between these two countries is a development that will advance the movement towards a multi-polar world order. 

**Global Economy:** The global economy has been severely impacted by the US war on Iran. The rising cost of energy has not only affected people's cost of living but has also led to shortages of fertilizers and food. Many countries in Africa, particularly in sub-Saharan Africa, are facing intensified hunger and starvation. According to UN estimates, 45 million people could be pushed into acute hunger due to rising food prices, and as a result, the overall number of people suffering from acute hunger will rise to 363 million – a historic high. 

4

On the other hand, a dominant section of the US economy is benefiting from the war – the oil companies. They stand to receive a windfall of more than $60 billion this year if crude prices maintain the levels they have hit since the start of the Iran war. It is estimated that American producers will generate an extra $5 billion cash flow in March alone following a roughly 47 per cent rise in oil prices since the conflict began. 

The war has adversely affected economic growth and increased inflation across countries. The IMF forecasts reduced growth of 3.1 percent in 2026 and projects inflation to rise to 4.4 percent. It has warned that if the war continues for a longer period, the global economy will fall into recession and inflation will reach nearly 6 percent. The economies of emerging and developing countries are likely to suffer twice as much as developed economies. 

Global trade is also seeing a significant recalibration. Global trade has declined by 2.1 percent in March, as compared to February. All major international financial institutions have lowered their global trade growth forecasts. US imports from China and Canada have decreased, while imports from Mexico, Vietnam, and Taiwan Province have increased. Similarly, China's exports have been reoriented from the US to Europe and other Asian countries. Countries are rushing to conclude trade deals. The EU concluded a trade deal with MERCOSUR, which had been pending for many years. The EU also concluded a trade deal with India. On the other hand, India concluded a deal with New Zealand. These economic partnerships show an urgency to protect their respective economies from the impact of the current uncertainties, which are expected to continue. 

The war is also contributing to the increased shift towards the usage of electric vehicles, alternative sources of power generation and a trade in related goods. 

# **Electoral Gains of the Right** 

**Hungary:** The defeat of Viktor Orban in Hungary’s 2026 election came from a combination of economic decline, political fatigue, corruption scandals, and generational change. Many Hungarians felt their living standards were deteriorating due to high inflation, a sharp rise in housing costs, rising energy prices, stagnant wages, and a weakened public sector. Orban was an authoritarian representative of the far-right. Anger among the people against these policies was reflected in high voter turnout. Many younger Hungarians were frustrated with corruption and lack of opportunity. These factors resulted in the victory of Peter Magyar, who was a former associate of Orban and also a fellow conservative. 

The defeat of Orban, though a welcome relief, the victory of Magyar does not generate optimism, as he too is a representative of the right-wing. Unlike Orban, Magyar will be aligning with the European Union and its geopolitical outlook, particularly in taking a position against Russia. 

**Britain:** The elections to local bodies in Britain saw a humiliating defeat for the Labour Party. The far-right Reform emerged as a dominant political force in these 

5

elections. The Conservatives were pushed to a distant third. Greens also registered significant victories. Labour lost almost 1,500 seats, the Tories lost 563, and Reform gained more than 1,400 seats, winning areas once considered safe seats for Labour or Conservative. 

Many Labour Party MPs are demanding the resignation of Prime Minister Keir Starmer, whose economic policies have become extremely unpopular. The Labour Party lost its support among the youth, working class, and progressive voters. While Reform alarmingly grew among the working class, the Greens received support from younger and progressive voters. The results show that the traditional two-party system is under stress from the challenge posed by the far-right Reform and the Greens. 

**Japan:** Japan’s far-right ruling Liberal Democrat Party (LDP), led by Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi, has won 352 of 465 seats in Japan's House of Representatives. Takaichi has pushed to toughen the immigration system, review rules around foreign ownership of Japanese land, and tackle any non-payments of tax and health insurance by foreign nationals. She is deliberately targeting foreign nationals who constitute only 3 percent of the population. Under the LDP, Japan is stridently marching towards militarisation and is targeting China with its nationalistic rhetoric. Japan is more closely allied with the US under the LDP. 

Another dangerous feature of these elections is the growth of extreme right Sanseito among the urban regions. Its agenda is anti-women, anti-science, and aggressively militaristic. On the other hand, the Japanese Communist Party (JCP) is steadily losing its base and vote share. In the present election, it could secure only 6 percent of the vote and lost in many of its traditional strongholds. 

**Nepal:** In Nepal, the Rastritya Swatantra Party (RSP) won 182 out of the 275 seats in the Parliamentary elections held on 5 March. It had nearly won two-thirds of the seats. The Communist Party of Nepal (Unified Marxist-Leninist) and the Nepali Congress suffered humiliating defeats in these elections, held after the mass protests. The Nepali Congress came in second with 38 seats, the CPN-UML was third with 25 seats and the Nepali Communist Party secured 17 seats, coming fourth. The elections mark a collapse of trust in traditional parties and the election of popular leaders who led the youth protests in 2025. This vote is a reflection of the people’s anger against the failure of the traditional parties to resolve the people’s issues. This is reflected in the election of a large number of candidates who are below 40 years of age and who are nationalistic. 

The Balen Shah headed RSP government passed an ordinance banning trade unions and student unions in the country. In name of clearing roads, the government launched an anti-encroachment drive throughout the country, which received widespread opposition as there was no alternative rehabilitation plan. The Supreme Court of Nepal stayed all these decisions. These measures of the government within 100 days of assuming office, clearly point towards its class biases. 

6

**Bangladesh:** In the parliamentary elections held on February 12, the Bangladesh Nationalist Party (BNP) emerged victorious with an overwhelming majority. The failure of the far-right Jamaat-e-Islami to win the elections is a positive feature. However, it won around 31 per cent of the vote share and substantially increased its seats. This is a threat that cannot be ignored. The Left parties, including the Communist Party of Bangladesh (CPB), contested the elections as an alliance but could not win any seats. This is a setback for the Left and progressive forces. 

Secular, democratic forces on both sides of the border should be vigilant and ensure that the ascendant communal forces do not create tensions between the people of both our countries. Border management is going to be crucial in this context. 

# **Class Resistance to the Attacks** 

**Unrest in Bolivia:** The newly elected president of Bolivia is reversing the policies adopted by the earlier MAS administration of Evo Morales and even his own election promises. The people of Bolivia have risen against the government demanding an increase in wages, a change in the land classification policy and better living standards. They are demanding the president to step down from office. Indigenous people enforced a road blockade preventing goods from reaching the capital city. Workers, employees and all other sections of the society too joined these protests. These protests signify a momentous development in a country, which started aligning closely with the US and Israel and implementing policies benefiting the elite capitalist class. The protests are a reflection of people’s anger against such policies. They are demanding the government to share power with indigenous groups, address the concerns of the working class by increasing their wages and taxing the rich. 

**Working Class Struggles:** In the last four months, there were many working class struggles across the globe. The May Day observations in various countries were used to express their anger against ruling classes. 

**United States:** May Day protests spread across dozens of US cities under the banner ‘May Day Strong’. Demonstrators organised ‘no work, no school, no shopping’ actions in New York, Chicago, San Francisco, Los Angeles and Washington, DC. 

**Europe:** May Day protests were especially intense in France, Italy, Portugal, Greece and Turkey. In Paris, demonstrators opposed proposals to weaken Labour Day protections and against corporate profits during the energy crisis. In Italy, general strikes were held in transportation and public sector. In Istanbul, May Day demonstrations were brutally suppressed by the police. In Portugal, one of the largest industrial actions was held during this period. In Greece, workers organised huge demonstrations against war and also against austerity measures. 

**Latin America:** In Argentina, unions mobilised against labour reforms proposed by President Javier Milei. The reforms aimed to make hiring and firing easier and 

7

restrict strikes, provoking strong union resistance and wide industrial action. In Chile, Colombia and Venezuela worker protests focused on inflation, wages, and inequality. 

**Asia-Pacific:** Workers rallied in Thailand, Philippines, South Korea and Indonesia on demands of higher minimum wages, stronger labour protections and lower living costs. In Manila, Philippines, protesters clashed with police near the US embassy during anti-war and labour demonstrations. 

Though these working class actions have varied by country, there are several common themes: (i) rising living costs and inflation; (ii) opposition to labour-law reforms; (iii) wage stagnation; (iv) energy-price shocks linked to the Iran war; (v) anti-austerity and anti-privatisation demands; and (vi) growing anger at inequality and ruling class parties. 

# **National** 

The major trends of the National developments during this period are: 

- i. The assembly election results further consolidated the hold of the RSS-BJP over the political system; 

- ii. The victory of BJP in Bengal is a major blow to the Left, progressive and secular forces; 

- iii. State institutions like the ECI are openly carrying forward the RSS agenda, undermining the sanctity of the electoral process; 

- iv. SIR is weaponised to permanently alienate and exclude certain sections of the society; 

- v. Workers’ spontaneous protests demonstrate the latent discontent brewing among them; 

- vi. The attitude of the Congress is harming the unity of the opposition parties in the fight against RSS/BJP; 

- vii.Contrary to government claims, Indian economy is on a very weak footing, with rising inflation, falling value of the rupee and lack of investments 

**Assembly Election Results:** Assembly elections were held in five states – Assam, Kerala, Puducherry, Tamilnadu, and West Bengal – in April. All the states witnessed huge voter turnouts. The SIR exercise, along with the fear that voters might lose their right to vote if they did not cast their ballots in these elections, also contributed to the high voter turnout, among various other reasons. 

The two major features of the assembly election results are the serious setback to the Left Democratic Front (LDF) in Keralam and the victory of the BJP in West Bengal. The DMK-led alliance also suffered a setback in Tamilnadu, where the 

8

TVK led by Vijay emerged as the single largest party. The BJP was able to retain Assam and, along with the NR Congress, once again win in Puducherry. 

In West Bengal and Assam, the results show the ascendancy of right-wing communal forces led by the BJP, which is a matter of deep concern for all secular, progressive and democratic forces. 

**Kerala:** In Kerala, the overall voting saw a marginal increase in 2026. The total polling percentage was 78.03. The LDF was defeated in Keralam after ten years of continuous rule. The LDF could secure 37.44 percent of the votes in 2026, as against 44.44 percent it secured in 2021. The UDF vote share increased from 38.88 percent in 2021 to 46.55 percent in 2026. The LDF won only 35 seats in 2026, as against 99 which it had won in 2021. The UDF increased its seat tally from 40 in 2021 to 102 in 2026. The BJP was able to win three seats, while it had none in 2021. In six seats, it was second. 

The CPI(M)'s vote share fell from 25.38 percent in 2021 to 21.77 percent in 2026, while the Congress vote share increased from 25.12 percent to 28.79 percent in the same period. Our vote share fell to its lowest in the past forty years and is a matter of serious concern. 

The Party has to self-critically review the reasons that led to the defeat of the LDF in Keralam. The state committee of Keralam is in the process of collecting opinions from various levels of the Party to understand the reasons for the setback. All these opinions will be discussed threadbare in the state secretariat and state committee meetings that will be held from 5-8 June 2026 in Thiruvanantapuram. A team of Polit Bureau members will attend these meetings. On the basis of these discussions, corrective measures for the identified weaknesses will be undertaken, taking into confidence the entire Party. 

The conduct of the Congress, making baseless allegations and spreading canards against the CPI(M), along with its soft approach towards communal forces, contributed to the growth of the BJP in Kerala, which could not be effectively exposed. 

**West Bengal:** The voting in West Bengal saw a steep increase from 82.32 percent in 2021 to 92.47 percent in 2026. In absolute terms, this is an increase of more than 34 lakh votes polled. The Left Front contested these elections along with the CPI(ML) Liberation and the ISF. The Congress contested these elections alone. 

In 2021, the TMC won 215 seats with a vote share of 48.59 percent. In these elections, it could win only 81 seats, securing 41.18 percent of the votes. The BJP won 77 seats in 2021 with a vote share of 37.97 percent, while it could win 206 seats with a vote share of 45.84 percent in 2026. Even though the vote share of the Congress fell marginally from 3.03 percent (2021) to 2.97 percent (2026), it succeeded in winning two seats, while it had none earlier. The Left Front secured 5.66 percent of the votes in 2021, which fell to 4.99 percent in 2026. 

9

Our Party contested 139 seats in 2021 and secured 4.71 percent of the votes. In these elections, we contested 195 seats and secured 4.45 percent of the votes. We were able to win one seat, Domkal, in these elections and thus ensure our presence in the legislative assembly. 

The victory of the BJP in West Bengal is a major blow and has been achieved on the basis of a communal, divisive and vitriolic hate campaign, the huge amount of money they spent, and the gross misuse of central agencies, including the Election Commission of India (ECI) and the SIR exercise. The BJP also benefited from the strong anti-incumbency against the corrupt and authoritarian TMC government. The danger of the ascendancy of BJP and its impact on the democratic structure of the society and peoples’ livelihood could not be thoroughly exposed. 

Our Party was able to campaign in many areas in which we were earlier forcibly prevented from campaigning. Many of our cadre, particularly youngsters, actively participated in the campaign. The various activities and campaigns conducted by all our class and mass organisations also helped in our election campaign. Despite all our efforts, we were unable to convert all the goodwill generated by the campaign into votes, as people are not confident that we are sufficiently strong enough to defeat the corrupt-authoritarian TMC. 

In West Bengal, all the Party units will meet and submit their opinions to the state committee, which will meet and finalise the review by the end of June. 

**Tamilnadu:** Tamilnadu too witnessed a substantial increase in the voting percentage in these elections. In 2021, the voter turnout was 73.63 percent, which increased to 84.69 percent in 2026. 

The ruling DMK alliance lost the elections to a new party, Tamilaga Vetriga Kazhagam TVK, which was formed by actor C. Joseph Vijay. The DMK won 133 seats in 2021 but could only win 59 in 2026. Its vote share too saw a fall from 46.45 percent in 2021 to 32.17 percent in these elections. The AIADMK-BJP alliance stood third in these elections. The BJP, which had 4 seats in 2021, was able to win only one seat in 2026, though its vote share saw a marginal increase (from 2.62 percent to 2.97 percent). 

In 2021, our Party contested six seats as part of the DMK-led alliance and won two seats. This time, we contested five seats and won two seats. Our vote share, however, fell from 0.85 percent in 2021 to 0.6 percent in 2026. 

The Governor of Tamil Nadu, an RSS cadre, flouting constitutional norms, tried to deny the leader of TVK, the largest party, the opportunity to form the government. This once again shows how the BJP tried to undermine election results by using the position of the Governor, even after TVK secured letters of support from the Left and also from the Congress. The VCK and the IUML also sent their letter of support to the TVK, which ultimately forced the Governor to invite TVK to form the government. 

10

While both the CPI(M) and CPI stated that they would support the government from outside, the Congress decided to join the government. A section of the AIADMK legislators split and also extended support to the TVK. 

**Assam:** In Assam, 85.38 percent polling was registered, which is an increase of 3.34 percent compared to 2021. The BJP was able to win the election with an increased majority and vote share. In 2021, it won 60 seats with a vote share of 33.21 percent. In 2026, it could win 82 seats with a vote share of 37.81 percent. 

The main opposition party, Congress, once again failed to win the confidence of the people. While its vote share remained more or less the same, its seats have seen a fall. In 2021, it won 29 seats and a 29.67 percent vote share, while in 2026, it could win only 19 seats and 29.84 percent of the votes. 

Our Party had seat adjustments with opposition parties, including the Congress. In 2021, we contested two seats and won one, securing 0.84 percent of the votes. In these elections, we contested two seats and failed to retain the seat. Our vote share too saw a fall and declined to 0.47 percent. 

The delimitation of constituencies undertaken by the BJP government had changed the demography of many seats. This exercise was used by the BJP to secure its hold and continue to win elections. Our sitting constituency too was affected by the delimitation, as some of our strongholds are no longer part of it. This is also one of the factors that contributed to our defeat. 

**Puducherry:** The voting percentage in Puducherry too saw an increase from 83.38 in 2021 to 89.83 in 2026. The NR Congress is the main party, with which BJP had an alliance, and won in these elections. Though the vote share of both the NR Congress and BJP decreased, they were able to retain power as they could maintain their majority by together winning 16 seats. In 2021, the NR Congress secured 25.85 percent of the votes, which fell to 23.12 percent in 2026. Similarly, the BJP vote share too fell from 13.66 percent in 2021 to 12.19 percent in 2026. The NR Congress increased its seats from 10 to 12, while the BJP seats were reduced from 6 to 4. 

An independent supported by our Party won from Mahe. In the last elections, we could not win any seat. Our vote share too saw a small increase. 

The Party must undertake an in-depth analysis of the reasons behind the continued growth of the BJP and how it is utilising the State machinery to achieve its objectives. Only such an analysis will help us devise correct tactics to confront and stop the rise of RSS-BJP-led Hindutva communal forces. 

The state committees where elections were held are in the process of conducting a review of the elections. The Central Committee will meet from July 11-13, 2026 and on the basis of all the state reviews, it will carry out a thorough review of the results. Rectification of the identified weaknesses will be immediately undertaken from top to bottom.

**INDIA Bloc:** The attitude of the Congress towards the fellow opposition parties is not helping in building unity of secular forces. Immediately after the recently concluded Assembly elections in Tamilnadu, it broke with the DMK and joined the TVK government. The DMK was upset with this decision and wrote to the Speaker of the Lok Sabha, asking for separate seating arrangements. Even during the Bihar elections, it behaved in a high-handed manner, which irked the RJD. The comments made by top-most leaders of the Congress, including the Leader of the Opposition, against our Party during the Keralam Assembly elections need to be condemned. They have maliciously alleged our Party, which has unblemished secular credentials, of entering into a deal with the BJP. Such behaviour does not help in the united fight against the RSS-BJP, which is the need of the hour. 

Our Party will continue to coordinate with the INDIA bloc in the Parliament. We will strive to mobilise Left, democratic and secular forces in the struggle against Hindutva communalism, the misuse of State institutions for promoting the agenda of the RSS-BJP and against attacks on the democratic rights of the people. 

**Delimitation:** The BJP-led Union government tried to push its agenda of changing the electoral map of the country in the name of carrying out the delimitation exercise. It linked the women’s reservation with delimitation and sought to win support. The opposition collectively defeated the BJP's designs, as the proposal required a constitutional amendment and hence a two-thirds majority. The BJP tried to project the entire opposition as anti-women but could not succeed. Though the opposition was able to push back the BJP’s efforts this time, the danger of delimitation persists. The way the entire exercise is proposed is intended to benefit the BJP electorally, particularly to gain sufficient majority in the parliament to amend and alter the Constitution. It is also intended to decrease the influence of southern states, where the BJP continues to have a weak presence. This once again demonstrates that the BJP is now effectively using State institutions to implement its agenda. 

We need to demand the implementation of the women’s reservation starting from the next round of elections. There is no need to link it with the delimitation exercise. 

**Impact of SIR:** The SIR exercise, initiated by the Election Commission of India (ECI) during the Bihar assembly elections, had a huge impact on the recently concluded assembly elections in West Bengal. The ECI introduced a new category, ‘logical discrepancies’, which was used to delete nearly 2.7 million names from the electoral rolls, even after these voters appealed and furnished all appropriate documentary evidence. Various studies are reporting the impact such deletions had on the electoral outcome. Never in the history of independent India have electoral rolls been tampered with to ensure the interests of a particular political party. The entire exercise undermines the credibility of our election system. Using their cadre, who have penetrated almost all echelons of state institutions, the RSS-BJP is trying to achieve its objectives. The appointment of an two officers who played an active 

12

role in conducting elections into the post of advisor to the newly sworn-in BJP Chief Minister of Bengal and Chief Secretary, is proof of such penetration. The ECI's role in pushing ahead with the SIR without listening to the various parties that are critical of the process is a clear example of this. 

The ECI announced its plans to conduct SIR in 17 more states. The Party’s position on the SIR is that no genuine citizen should be denied of their right to vote. The ECI is crossing the line drawn on its powers through the SIR and unfortunately, the Supreme Court has not given the verdict on the validity of this process. 

**SIR and Attack on Beneficiaries:** The BJP governments in Bihar and Bengal announced that those whose names are deleted from the electoral rolls in the SIR process will not be allowed to access government benefits. The Bihar government announced that five lakh ration cards have already been withdrawn on the basis of SIR. It is established that lakhs of genuine citizens were deleted from the voter lists through the SIR process, despite them showing various documentary evidence.
