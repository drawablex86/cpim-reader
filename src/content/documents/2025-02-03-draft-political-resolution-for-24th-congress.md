# **Draft** 

**Political Resolution for 24**<sup>**th**</sup> **Congress (Adopted at the Central Committee Meeting held on January 17-19, 2025 at Kolkata)** 

## **Introduction** 

- 1 **0.1** The period since the 23<sup>rd</sup> Congress was marked by a growing 2 conflict between the forces of the Hindutva-corporate regime, 3 represented by the Modi government and the secular-democratic 4 forces who were opposed to it. The push to impose a reactionary 5 Hindutva agenda and the authoritarian drive to suppress the 6 opposition and democracy displays neo-fascist characteristics. 7 The CPI(M) and the Left parties consistently opposed the 8 communal-corporate policies of the Central government. 9 Workers, peasants and other sections of the working people 10 waged united struggles to defend their livelihoods and economic 11 rights. 

- 1 **0.2** The Lok Sabha election of May 2024 resulted in a setback 2 for the BJP; it lost its majority in the Lok Sabha and faces a 3 strengthened opposition in Parliament. However, the third term 4 of the Modi government is seeing a continuation of the Hindutva 5 neo-liberal agenda of the BJP-RSS. The results have opened 6 up space for wider resistance to the pro-corporate Hindutva 7 policies of the Modi government, both outside and inside the 8 Parliament.

Foundational agreements 3 for military collaboration have been completed and it is 4 increasingly aligned with US geopolitical interests in the Asia5 Pacific region, as seen in its participation in the QUAD and its 6 shameful support for Israel’s genocidal war on Gaza. All these 7 developments must be seen in the context of global developments 8 and geopolitical relations, which have a direct bearing on our 9 national situation.

# **Israeli Genocide in Palestine** 

- 1 **1.2** Israel carried on its genocidal onslaught on Gaza for fifteen 2 months after the Hamas attack on October 7, 2023. Until 3 the ceasefire was announced, 47,035 Palestinians were 4 killed and a hundred thousand injured in the brutal Israeli 5 aggression. Nearly 60 per cent of those killed are women 6 and children. Israel violated all international laws with 7 impunity by attacking schools, hospitals, UN refugee 8 shelters and unarmed civilians. It used drones, banned 9 weapons and carried out targeted assassinations. With a 10 complicit US administration and its allies, Israel intends to 11 occupy the West Bank.

- 1 **1.6** The fall of Bashar al-Assad’s regime in Syria will have 2 major repercussions. - 1 **1.21 Attacks on the Working Class:** The effects of rising 2 commodity prices are severely felt by the working class, as 3 their real wages did not increase. A total of 791 million 4 workers have seen their wages fail to keep up with inflation 5 and, as a result, have lost $1.5 trillion over the last two 6 years, equivalent to nearly a month (25 days) of lost wages 7 for each worker. In 8 2023, 10.7 per cent of the world population (864.1 million 9 people) was affected by severe food insecurity. Nearly 60 10 per cent of the people who go hungry are women and girls. 11 The climate breakdown is also contributing to an increase 12 in global inequality.

# **Growth of the Far-Right** 

- 1 **1.31** Exploiting genuine concerns about people’s deteriorating 2 living conditions, neo-fascist forces and far-right parties 3 are gaining ground in some countries. They have benefited 4 by rousing feelings of racism, xenophobia and fear of 5 immigration. # **Climate Change** 

- 1 **1.35** Many of the far-right parties in developed countries that 2 were elected to government are also climate-change deniers, 3 which will negatively affect action to meet the climate 4 challenge. The period from January to September 2024 has 5 been the first almost year-long period in which the global 6 average temperature stayed 1.5° Celsius or more above the 7 pre-industrial average, threatening to breach the desired 8 lower limit set in the Paris Agreement of 2015 for restricting 9 global warming. The CO2 emissions of the top 1 per cent 10 exceed those of the poorest two-thirds of the world, or five 11 billion people. On the other hand, it is the poor and 12 vulnerable of the Global South who are, and will be, the 13 worst affected. 

**14**

- 1 **1.36** The rich, developed countries are not ready to own up to 2 their responsibilities in contributing to the fight against 3 climate change and for the protection of the environment. 4 Developed countries are persisting with the use of fossil 5 fuels, reducing them slowly, while pushing developing 6 countries to adopt expensive renewable energy sources and 7 attempting to restrict their industrial growth and the 8 provision of equitable energy access to their populations. 9 At COP29, which concluded in Baku in 2024, the developed 10 countries offered only $300 billion to be fully available by 11 2035, as climate finance for developing countries. This so12 called ‘deal’ is a pittance that poorer countries have been 13 forced to accept and is a disaster for our planet and 14 communities who are being threatened by flooding, 15 starvation and displacement due to global warming. Instead 16 of providing adequate finance, the developed countries wish 17 to commodify nature further, putting the burden of reducing 18 emissions on the shoulders of small farmers and rural 19 populations. Even the modest gains in global food security 20 are threatened by the push towards a shift to crop production 21 for bio-fuels and energy, which will displace production 22 for food and nutrition. 

- 1 **1.37** Wealthy countries responsible for over 75 per cent of global 2 accumulated emissions since the start of industrialisation 3 have a responsibility to support developing countries and 4 pay them for climate adaptation, the loss and damage caused 5 by the impacts of climate disasters and for a path of 6 development, moving away in a just and equitable manner 7 from fossil fuel dependence to societies based on renewable 8 energy and climate resilience. Rich and developed countries 9 owe trillions of dollars in climate debt annually as 10 reparations to the developing countries. Left and progressive 11 forces should join hands to pressurise advanced capitalist 12 countries to commit to deeper emission cuts and honour 13 their commitments on climate finance and technology 14 transfer.
